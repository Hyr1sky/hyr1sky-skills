# Project Narrative Builder

Shape project truth into a communication experience for a specific audience and decision. Own content selection, argument, translation, and evidence placement; let document, slide, or web skills own final rendering when needed.

## Boundary

- Start from established facts or a clearly identified source set. If facts are missing, state the research gap instead of inventing a smoother story.
- Use `research-codebase-to-wiki` when the primary task is to discover and document codebase facts.
- Use artifact-specific skills for polished slides, documents, websites, or diagrams after the narrative is settled.
- Do not activate for generic sentence polishing when audience, decision, and project framing are irrelevant.

## Workflow

1. **Define the communication job**
   - Identify the audience and the decision, belief, or action the communication should enable.
   - Determine whether the audience needs to learn, perform a task, look up facts, build understanding, review, or decide. Do not combine these jobs by default.
   - Capture prior knowledge, incentives, concerns, objections, evidence threshold, vocabulary, available attention, and format constraints.
   - If the audience is underspecified, infer a provisional audience brief from context and label it; ask only when a wrong assumption would change the narrative.

2. **Establish the truth set**
   - Gather claims, evidence, uncertainties, constraints, tradeoffs, and known objections from the supplied project material.
   - Separate `verified`, `reported`, `inferred`, and `aspirational` statements.
   - Preserve negative evidence and limitations that affect audience trust or the requested decision.

3. **Apply the relevance filter**
   - Include a fact because it changes what this audience understands or decides, not merely because it is interesting.
   - Translate or defer details that require too much prerequisite knowledge.
   - Choose examples and comparisons from the audience's world without distorting the system.
   - For complex audiences or deliverables, read [references/audience-and-argument.md](references/audience-and-argument.md).

4. **Build the argument**
   - State the governing point early.
   - Create a causal arc appropriate to the job: context and stakes, current tension, insight, approach, evidence, tradeoffs, and requested next step.
   - Anticipate the audience's strongest reasonable objection and answer it with proportionate evidence.
   - Use detail in layers so a reader can stop early without misunderstanding the conclusion.

5. **Produce the narrative package**
   - Deliver an audience brief, one-sentence governing thought, argument map, key proof points, objection handling, vocabulary choices, and a recommended sequence.
   - Draft the requested text when useful, but distinguish the reusable narrative blueprint from format-specific copy.
   - When several audiences matter, create separate variants rather than a compromised average voice.

## Quality Checks

- Can the intended audience identify within the opening what this is, why it matters to them, and what they should do or believe?
- Does every major claim have the level and type of evidence this audience requires?
- Are technical simplifications faithful to responsibilities, causality, scale, and uncertainty?
- Is the narrative centered on audience stakes instead of the project team's effort chronology?
- Does the structure match the audience's communication job rather than a habitual report template?
- Are limitations placed where they improve decision quality rather than hidden in a disclaimer?

Never use narrative force to convert an aspiration into a fact.
